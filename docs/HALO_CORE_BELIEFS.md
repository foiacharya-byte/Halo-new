# HALO — CORE BELIEFS
## The document that turns research into conviction
*Distilled only from `HALO_AWARD_RESEARCH_REPORT.md`, `HALO_EMOTIONAL_JOBS_TO_BE_DONE.md`, and the underlying market/Reddit research. No design, UI, features, or implementation — beliefs only.*

---

## How to read this

Nine beliefs, merged from dozens of findings. Each is rated:
- **Proven** — strongly evidenced; safe to build on.
- **Strong hypothesis** — good evidence in adjacent domains; unproven for Halo specifically.
- **Weak hypothesis** — plausible, logically coherent, largely untested.

The point of this file is **conviction with honesty about its limits.** Where a belief is shaky, that is stated, not hidden. The forced prioritisation (A/B/C) at the end is the real output.

> **The spine all nine hang from:** Barodians are not short of *listings*. They are short of *trust* — a way to know, before they commit, that they won't be made a fool, in a city they want to feel is theirs.

---

## BELIEF 1 — The problem is real and current, not invented

**The belief:** There is a genuine, painful, daily trust vacuum in how Barodians find local services — Halo is answering a real need, not manufacturing one.

**Why we believe it:** Every other belief is worthless if the problem isn't real. This is the foundation, so it must be the most evidenced.

**Evidence:** The no-show / advance-taken-and-vanished economy (the painhook content, culturally verified); 68% of Indians have encountered fake reviews and 42% distrust review authenticity (LocalCircles); 91% trust the neighbour's word over any platform (Nielsen); post-flood institutional distrust is live (ministers heckled, "man-made disaster" narrative). The pre-commitment blindness, closed recommendation economy, and no-memory problems are structural, not anecdotal.

**Status:** **Proven** — that the *problem* exists. (Not proven that Halo is the answer; that's Beliefs 2–9.)

**What would disprove it:** Barodians, asked directly, report they already find trusted services easily and feel no pain — the WhatsApp-group / known-contact system works well enough that no gap is felt.

**Risk if wrong:** Total. A solution to a non-problem. But this is the lowest-risk belief in the set; the evidence is strong and convergent.

---

## BELIEF 2 — Halo is hired for a feeling, not a function

**The belief:** People reach for Halo to *stop feeling like a fool, an outsider, or someone with no one to ask* — the directory is the cover story; the emotional job is the engine.

**Why we believe it:** The functional jobs (find an electrician) read as thin and commoditised; the emotional jobs (don't let me be naive, let me belong, let me be the one with the contact) are where intensity, loyalty, and word-of-mouth concentrate.

**Evidence:** JTBD analysis ranks emotional jobs A1/A2/A3/A4 above every functional task on intensity × deficit; self-brand connection research (loyalty peaks when a product is absorbed into self-concept); place-attachment → word-of-mouth; the painhook's resonance is emotional ("haa, aa to rojnu chhe"), not informational.

**Status:** **Strong hypothesis.**

**What would disprove it:** Users behave purely transactionally — they extract one number, never return, never share, never use identity/belonging language about Halo — and a colder, more *complete* listing utility out-retains it.

**Risk if wrong:** We over-invest in emotion and identity and lose to a faster, more comprehensive, emotionally-flat directory. We'd have built a beautiful thing people use once.

---

## BELIEF 3 — Credible trust is specific, named, and honest about its gaps — not aggregated, anonymous, and perfect

**The belief:** The neighbour's concrete, named word *with its blemishes* beats the anonymous aggregated 5-star average; admitting what wasn't verified builds more trust than claiming perfection.

**Why we believe it:** This is Halo's actual product differentiator. It merges two findings that point the same way: trust comes from specificity + identity, and from honesty about limitations.

**Evidence:** The blemishing / two-sided-message effect (an admitted flaw raises believability; a flawless 5.0 reads as fake); humans detect fake reviews at chance level (~50.8%), so *perceived* credibility cues — reviewer identity, balanced detail, concrete reasoning — are everything; Stanford Web Credibility (real-world specificity, named humans); 68% fake-review distrust has poisoned aggregate stars; Oslo's honest self-deprecation and Front Porch Forum's transparency both outperformed polished alternatives.

**Status:** **Strong hypothesis.** (The general principle is well-evidenced; the *strong prescription* — that Halo should largely abandon the 5-star paradigm it inherited — is the riskier edge of it.)

**What would disprove it:** In testing, users sort and decide by star average, ignore named vouches, and trust *less* when "unverified / verifying" is shown prominently — i.e., honesty about gaps lowers confidence and action rather than raising it.

**Risk if wrong:** We deliberately ship a humble, blemish-showing, star-light product that reads as *incomplete and weak* against confident, badge-heavy competitors — and lose on first impression.

## BELIEF 4 — The heritage identity is a strategic asset, not a liability

**The belief:** Halo's warm, rooted, heritage character (paper, serif, craft, calm) is the contrarian-correct position in 2025–26 — "modern/new-gen" gloss would erase its one defensible difference.

**Why we believe it:** The instinct to chase a contemporary tech look would walk Halo into the exact aesthetic the field is now punishing.

**Evidence:** Award-craft research — the 2025–26 winner set rewards warm/paper/serif/grain/cultural-reference and the cliché "punish list" is gradients, glassmorphism, AI-gloss, beige-startup minimalism; the heritage direction was the right bet *before* it was fashionable.

**Status:** **Strong hypothesis — contested.** The award/trend evidence is solid, but two cautions are real: (a) trends move, and (b) the founder's own gut read the current execution as "boring," which is a signal that *the audience may not share the jury's taste.*

**What would disprove it:** Target Barodians consistently describe the identity as dated, boring, or *less* trustworthy than a cleaner modern look — and engagement/trust improves materially when the aesthetic changes. (Critical distinction to test: does it read as *boring*, or merely as *under-powered* — those have opposite fixes.)

**Risk if wrong:** We defend an aesthetic the actual users don't respond to, mistaking award-jury approval for resident love — the exact "awards ≠ Vadodara-love" trap the research itself warns about.

## BELIEF 5 — Vadodara-specificity is the moat; the city's institutional-trust lineage is its deepest (and least-tested) form

**The belief:** What makes Halo "ours" and uncopyable is hyper-local *recognition precision* (your lane, your kitli, your area) — and, at its deepest, the framing that a community-trust platform *continues Vadodara's own 150-year tradition of institutions built on trusting ordinary people* (the Sayajirao lineage).

**Why we believe it:** Generic place-products fail; rooted ones become beloved. Specificity is also legally and competitively uncopyable — no rival can fake your city's memory.

**Evidence:** Recognition/emotional-precision drives nostalgia and sharing (meme-page behaviour, nostalgia research, @historyofvadodara's proven engagement); residents-first rootedness wins (Misato, Porto, Lokal); the Sayajirao history is *factually* verified (libraries, Bank of Baroda, Ambedkar's patronage).

**Status:** **Split.** Recognition-precision as a moat = **Strong hypothesis.** The institutional-trust *lineage narrative resonating with users* = **Weak hypothesis** — the history is proven, but that connecting Halo to it will *move* a Barodian (rather than read as pretentious or irrelevant in a service context) is untested.

**What would disprove it:** Hyper-local specificity produces no more attachment/sharing than generic framing; and the lineage story, when shown to Barodians, lands as grandiose, academic, or beside-the-point.

**Risk if wrong:** We build a narrative and positioning spine on a museum story users don't care about when they just want a plumber — earnest, self-important, and ignored.

## BELIEF 6 — Pride and contribution require stake-holding, and contribution must visibly succeed

**The belief:** People will contribute (add, vouch) primarily because *their* corner of the city is blank or wrong and they have a stake in fixing it — but only if the effort reliably lands; a contribution that vanishes into silence converts pride into resentment.

**Why we believe it:** Halo's entire bootstrapping and trust model depends on residents contributing. If they won't, the model fails regardless of everything else.

**Evidence:** Civic-pride research (pride requires the feeling you *played a part*); the strongest commons-contribution trigger is "the place you care about is blank/wrong on the map" (OSM studies); fun out-produces ideology (Wikipedia motivation); the IKEA effect (people over-value what they build) **with its hard boundary condition** — the effect reverses when the effort fails.

**Status:** **Strong hypothesis** (the *motivation* is well-evidenced; the *boundary condition* is proven and non-negotiable).

**What would disprove it:** Even when shown their area is empty, Barodians don't contribute at meaningful rates — the spectator-to-stakeholder conversion doesn't happen, and supply never bootstraps.

**Risk if wrong:** The community-built moat never forms; Halo stays a thin, founder-maintained list and the whole "by neighbours, for neighbours" premise is hollow.

---

## BELIEF 7 — Residents-first love, expressed as social-currency sharing, is the only affordable growth engine

**The belief:** Halo grows by making Barodians *fall in love and look good sharing it* — not by features, ads, scale tactics, or AI; optimize for the resident's pride-forward and reach follows.

**Why we believe it:** A solo founder on a low budget has no paid-distribution path. Organic, love-driven, word-of-mouth growth isn't a preference — it's the only viable channel.

**Evidence:** Sharing runs on social currency + high-arousal pride/awe (STEPPS; Berger & Milkman); place-attachment predicts word-of-mouth; love-loyalty produces more advocacy than switching-cost lock-in; Misato Town (pop. 4,600) won internationally *by optimising for residents falling in love first*; Lokal grew from a WhatsApp group on vernacular usefulness, not marketing budget.

**Status:** **Strong hypothesis.**

**What would disprove it:** People find Halo useful but never forward it; sharing rates stay negligible; growth flatlines without paid acquisition — proving love doesn't convert to distribution at Halo's scale.

**Risk if wrong:** Beloved but tiny. Without organic spread, the budget reality means Halo never reaches critical mass, and a funded, colder competitor wins on distribution alone.

---

## BELIEF 8 — There are two users with opposite emotional jobs

**The belief:** Newcomers (students, NRIs, new brides) hire Halo for *orientation and belonging*; established locals hire it for *validation of what they already know and the social currency of sharing it* — conflating them weakens both.

**Why we believe it:** The two segments have measurably different relationships to the city's closed recommendation economy — one is locked out, one owns the keys.

**Evidence:** JTBD analysis (newcomer jobs A12/A2 vs local jobs A3/A14/A20); place-attachment predictors (attachment rises with length of residence and local ties — so newcomers and long-timers sit at opposite ends by definition).

**Status:** **Weak hypothesis** — logically sound, empirically untested for Halo.

**What would disprove it:** Both segments behave identically in what they value and how they use Halo; treating them as one serves both fine, and the distinction adds no insight.

**Risk if wrong (either way):** Over-segment and we split a tiny founder's focus prematurely; ignore it and we build a one-size message that resonates with neither. The risk is moderate — this is a refinement, not a foundation.

---

## BELIEF 9 — Demand intensity is not permission

**The belief:** The highest-demand, highest-deficit jobs — vetting domestic workers, payments/disputes, becoming an identity filter, emergency dispatch — are precisely the ones that would *destroy* Halo, ethically or existentially.

**Why we believe it:** The strongest market pull points straight at the cliff edge. This belief is a guardrail, not a growth bet.

**Evidence:** The "must never solve" analysis (§D of the JTBD doc): "verifying" domestic labour collapses into caste/community screening and false safety guarantees; identity-filtering is the Nextdoor/Ahmedabad-exclusion failure mode; payments/arbitration break "directory ≠ platform," contradict no-commission, and create liability; a flawless "safe" badge makes every failure Halo's fault and kills the honest-trust posture.

**Status:** **Conviction** (a values-and-risk principle, near-proven on downside grounds rather than a testable market hypothesis).

**What would disprove it:** A disciplined, narrow version of one of these (e.g., consent-based connect that *technically* enables booking) proves both safe *and* the only path to viability — i.e., purity turns out to be a luxury Halo can't afford.

**Risk if wrong:** A competitor serves the dangerous job "well enough" and captures the market while Halo's principled refusal becomes principled irrelevance. (Even so: this is the belief worth being wrong-and-safe on rather than right-and-harmful.)

# THE FORCED PRIORITISATION

## A. The 3 beliefs to bet the company on

These are highest-conviction *and* strategy-defining — what Halo *is*, how it *earns trust*, and how it *survives*:

1. **Belief 2 — Halo is hired for a feeling, not a function.** Get this wrong and every other choice is mis-aimed.
2. **Belief 3 — Credible trust is specific, named, and honest about its gaps.** This is the actual product, and the one thing competitors with money still can't easily copy.
3. **Belief 7 — Residents-first love → social-currency sharing is the only affordable growth engine.** Given the budget, there is no plan B for distribution.

*(Belief 1 underpins all three but is Proven, so it's a foundation, not a bet. Belief 9 is the guardrail around all three.)*

## B. The 3 beliefs to validate through the questionnaire

These are uncertain *and* answerable by asking/showing Barodians directly:

1. **Belief 3 — the unit of trust.** Ask: what would actually make you trust a listing — a named neighbour's specific account, a star average, or official verification? (We're betting the company on this, so it's the *first* thing to de-risk.)
2. **Belief 4 — the heritage identity.** Test whether the audience reads it as *rooted and trustworthy* or *dated and boring* — and crucially, separate "boring" from "under-powered."
3. **Belief 5 — Vadodara-specificity and the lineage narrative.** Test whether the institutional-trust story *moves* a Barodian or reads as pretentious/irrelevant — and whether hyper-local recognition outperforms generic framing.

## C. The 3 beliefs most likely to be wrong

Lowest-confidence convictions — where I'd bet *against* myself:

1. **Belief 5 (the lineage half).** A proven history doesn't guarantee an emotionally resonant narrative; "the city built on trust" may be a founder's romance the user doesn't feel when they just want a tutor.
2. **Belief 8 — two distinct users.** Coherent on paper, but may be over-segmentation that a tiny team can't afford and the data won't support.
3. **Belief 4 — heritage as asset.** The founder's own instinct already flagged it as boring; "contrarian-correct" may be cope, and award-jury taste is not resident love. This is the most internally *contested* belief in the set.

---

### Note on the deliberate overlap
Beliefs 4 and 5 appear in both B and C; Belief 3 appears in both A and B. This is intentional, not sloppy: **the beliefs most likely to be wrong are exactly the ones to test first, and the belief we're betting the company on is the one we most urgently need to de-risk.** Conviction and doubt are not opposites here — the questionnaire exists to convert the shakiest high-stakes beliefs into either evidence or a pivot.

> **The convergence, in one line:** Halo bets that Barodians will love and spread an honest, specific, rooted way to *not be made a fool in their own city* — and stakes nothing on guarantees, scale tactics, or the jobs that demand intensity is begging it to take.
