# HALO — THE CONVERSATION JOURNEY
## The emotional experience, designed before the questionnaire
*Built on `HALO_CORE_BELIEFS.md`, `HALO_QUESTIONNAIRE_STRATEGY.md`, and the hybrid decision (the branded journey + a separate blind lab). This designs the **felt experience**. The questionnaire is downstream: the questions are only the instrumentation each emotional beat already invites. Emotion first, questions never first.*

---

## 0. The principle this whole document obeys

> **A Barodian should finish this feeling they were *heard*, not *surveyed* — and certainly not *sold to*. The data is a by-product of a good conversation, not the purpose they can feel.**

If at any point it feels like a form or a funnel, we have failed — and the data degrades at the same moment the feeling does. The two goals (feel heard / research integrity) are not in tension here; a person who feels genuinely heard answers more honestly, not less.

---

## 1. The emotional arc (why the order is the order)

The six stages are a deliberate rise, not a checklist:

**Recognition → Dignity → Self-insight → Pride (peak) → Belonging → Ownership.**

- **Peak–end design.** The emotional peak is engineered at **Contribution** (Stage 4) — the moment of pride and generosity — and the **end** lands on belonging, not a form-submit. By the peak-end rule, this is what they'll remember.
- **Completion by commitment, not coercion.** Each stage asks slightly more than the last, *after* the previous one has already earned a small yes. By the time we ask anything real, they're already invested (recall → trust reflection → their own gift → the join). The contribution comes *before* the ask, so generosity primes the waitlist rather than the waitlist extracting it. Drop-off is fought with momentum and dignity, never with a progress bar.
- **No stage feels like the previous one's setup.** Each is its own emotional beat. The research rides inside the beats; it is never the beat itself.

---

## 2. The integrity firewall (how research survives a branded experience)

Three rules carry the entire research integrity, replacing brand-blindness without a cold form:

1. **Measure before you reveal.** Stages 2–4 (behavioural recall, trust discovery, contribution) happen **before Halo names itself or pitches anything** (Stage 5). Bias-sensitive measurement is therefore taken blind — the respondent can't flatter an answer toward a product they haven't met. Trust signals in Stage 3 are shown as *generic ways people show trust*, never as "Halo's badges."
2. **Count actions, not opinions.** Contribution and the waitlist join are *behaviours*. Behaviour can't be polite. These are the truest data points in the journey and they are impossible in a form.
3. **The aesthetic is not tested here — and can't be.** The respondent marinates in Halo's heritage look from the first second, so the journey cannot neutrally judge whether that look is *boring vs under-powered* (Belief 4). **B4 lives only in the separate blind lab.** Naming this protects us from pretending the journey settled something it structurally cannot.

> The landing wears Halo's *mood* (warm paper, serif, Gujarati) but withholds Halo's *name, product, and pitch* until Stage 5. The "reveal" is revealing **what this is and that you can join it** — not revealing the aesthetic.

## 3. THE SIX STAGES

### STAGE 1 — LANDING
*The emotional turn: from "another link" to "someone sees my city."*

**1. What the user is feeling:** Recognition and relief — *"this person actually gets it."* Disarmed, curious, a little seen. Crucially **not** "I'm about to fill out a survey" and **not** "I'm being marketed to." The guard drops.

**2. What Halo is learning:** Almost nothing explicit yet — and that restraint is the point. Ambiently: whether an unmistakably-Vadodara emotional hook earns the first step at all (drop-off here means the pain framing missed). This stage *buys the right* to ask anything later.

**3. Belief being tested:** Foundationally **B2** (that Halo is hired for a *feeling*, not a function — does emotional recognition, not a feature promise, earn the click). Sets the firewall by withholding the brand.

**4. The mistake that would ruin it:** Leading with Halo, a logo, a product, or a "help us improve / we value your feedback" survey preamble. Either move triggers the exact marketing-defense (and self-presentation bias) the journey is built to avoid — and a generic, pan-India, could-be-any-city framing breaks the "this is *mine*" spell before it forms. A visible "Question 1 of 25" would end it here.

---

### STAGE 2 — FIRST INTERACTION
*The emotional turn: from audience to consulted expert.*

**1. What the user is feeling:** *Dignity.* Being asked, treated as the authority on their own city, given an easy and even enjoyable first thing to say. The first effortless "yes" — momentum. *"I know this. I have things to tell you."*

**2. What Halo is learning:** The **behavioural baseline** — how Barodians *actually* find and trust local help today (the real status quo and the real competitor set: the neighbour, the society WhatsApp group, the distrusted listing site). Revealed behaviour, the anchor everything else is read against.

**3. Belief being tested:** Establishes the anchor for **B3**, and gives lived-behaviour evidence for **B1** (the pain is real in what they actually do). Primarily: the behavioural baseline.

**4. The mistake that would ruin it:** Opening with something hard, abstract, or evaluative — that turns dignity into a test and kills momentum. Asking *intention* ("what would you do?") instead of *recall* ("what did you do last time?"). Or any whiff of judging how they currently cope — the moment they feel graded, they perform instead of report.

---

### STAGE 3 — TRUST DISCOVERY
*The emotional turn: from answering to being understood.*

**1. What the user is feeling:** A small **"aha"** — realising, maybe for the first time, what actually makes *them* trust a stranger with their home or their child. This reads as being *understood at a deeper level*, not as being quizzed. Self-insight is pleasurable; it deepens the sense of being heard rather than spending it.

**2. What Halo is learning:** The core trust data — which signal actually moves them (a named, specific neighbour's account vs a star average vs an "official" badge vs bare facts), and the revealing gap between *what feels trustworthy* and *what makes them actually reach out*. Plus the blemish reaction: does honesty about what *wasn't* verified build or break trust. This is the most important measurement in the journey — and it is taken **blind**.

**3. Belief being tested:** **B3** — the unit of trust. The company-bet belief. This stage is its single best read.

**4. The mistake that would ruin it:** **Telegraphing Halo's preferred answer** — any hint that the named-neighbour vouch is "the right one" — which contaminates the crux measurement with demand bias. This is *the* firewall violation. Equally fatal: making it feel like a quiz with a correct answer (anxiety replaces insight), weighting one option visually, or letting the crux drag until the warmth curdles into work.

---

### STAGE 4 — CONTRIBUTION
*The emotional turn: from being heard to having something to give. **(The peak.)**

**1. What the user is feeling:** **Pride and generosity.** *"My Vadodara, my gem, my hard-won knowledge is worth something."* This is the emotional summit and the moment belonging crystallises — giving creates ownership in a way receiving never does. Warmth, a little delight, the quiet status of being the one who knows.

**2. What Halo is learning:** Whether people *will actually contribute* (the supply-side, bootstrapping question the whole model rests on) — measured as **behaviour**, not a promise. And recognition-precision in the wild: do they light up when naming *their specific* area, lane, kitli, gem? The texture of real local knowledge surfaces here.

**3. Belief being tested:** **B6** (stake-holding contribution — *and* its hard boundary condition: the effort must visibly land) and **B5a** (recognition precision). Both behavioural.

**4. The mistake that would ruin it:** Making the gift feel like **labour or data extraction** ("submit your details") instead of generosity. Asking for it *before* trust is earned (giving to a stranger feels like work). Making it *mandatory* — coercion annihilates a gift. And the IKEA-effect killer: letting the contribution **vanish without acknowledgement** — effort that doesn't visibly succeed converts the day's pride into resentment, the exact failure Belief 6 warns of.

---

### STAGE 5 — HALO REVEAL
*The emotional turn: from "my city" to "this is for us." The payoff, not the pitch.*

**1. What the user is feeling:** *"Oh — this is for **us**."* Recognition at city-scale; pride; **belonging clicks** (આપણું). Because they've already reflected and already given, the reveal lands as a *gift and a payoff* — "there's a name for the thing you've been describing, and it's yours" — rather than as an ad. It must feel **earned**, never like the previous four stages were bait.

**2. What Halo is learning:** Whether the identity and the institutional-trust lineage *land as pride* or *curdle into alienation* ("pretentious / academic / not for people like me") — measured both by reaction and, more honestly, by whether they **continue** toward the waitlist after the reveal (downstream behaviour beats "is it nice?").

**3. Belief being tested:** **B5b** — the lineage narrative — the weakest, most-likely-wrong belief, and the one this stage exists to settle. Measured by reaction *and* continuation.

**4. The mistake that would ruin it:** Turning the reveal into **marketing** — features, hype, "the #1 platform," "AI coming soon." That detonates the marketing-defense the whole journey disarmed, and retroactively reframes everything before it as a funnel — a betrayal that poisons both the relationship and the data. Equally: a **grandiose or academic** telling of the lineage (the alienation risk), or revealing too much *product* when the thing under test is the *story*. The reveal stays humble, short, and earned.

---

### STAGE 6 — WAITLIST
*The emotional turn: from "for us" to "I helped start this." Closure as belonging.*

**1. What the user is feeling:** **Ownership and anticipation.** *"I'm one of the first. I helped begin this. એ હાલો."* Joining should feel like stepping into something, not submitting a form. The journey ends on pride returned *to them*, not value extracted *from them*.

**2. What Halo is learning:** The truest single data point — **revealed intent** (did they actually join). Plus a side-light on whether newcomers and locals convert differently. The join is the honest behavioural proxy for "would you actually use this" that no survey question can buy.

**3. Belief being tested:** **B2 / B7** behaviourally (does the felt value convert into an *action* — does love move them to join), with a **B8** side-light (segment differences). The engineered ending of the peak-end arc.

**4. The mistake that would ruin it:** A cold, transactional *"enter your email"* with no emotional payoff — that collapses the peak and re-frames the whole journey as lead-gen. Or the opposite: **over-promising hype** ("launching soon, exclusive access!!") that betrays the honest posture the rest earned. Or demanding too much personal data (friction + a DPDP line Halo doesn't cross). The ending must hand the value back — *you helped start Vadodara's own* — not take it.

---

## 4. How this avoids the two deaths

**It avoids feeling like a survey by:** opening on recognition not a preamble; one thread at a time, conversational, in their language; framing every research moment as *being consulted about their own city*, not graded; no grids, no visible question-counter, no "feedback form" vocabulary. The research is the conversation's exhaust, never its face.

**It avoids feeling like marketing by:** withholding the brand and any pitch until Stage 5; centring *their* voice and *their* city, not Halo's claims; making the reveal a humble, earned payoff rather than a sell; and ending by returning value to them. No hype, no features, no urgency, no "AI coming soon."

## 5. The integrity ledger (belief × stage)

| **B3** — unit of trust | Stage 3 (Trust discovery) | Blind (pre-reveal) | The crux; signals shown generically, Halo's preference hidden |

**Bonus:** the journey hands us behavioural side-evidence on Beliefs 1, 2, 6, 7 — beliefs the *minimum study* scoped out — for free, because they show up as actions in a real experience rather than answers in a form.

---

## 6. What the questionnaire now is

Given this journey, "the questionnaire" is no longer a document to author — it's the **minimum instrumentation each beat already invites**: a behavioural-recall prompt (Stage 2), a neutral trust trade-off and a blemish reaction (Stage 3), an open invitation to give (Stage 4), a reaction-and-continue moment (Stage 5), a join (Stage 6). The questions, when we write them, must *fit the feeling that's already there* — never insert a feeling the stage doesn't have. If a question would break the emotional turn of its stage, the question is wrong, not the journey.

> **One line:** Design the conversation so a Barodian feels recognised, consulted, understood, generous, and finally part of something — and the honest answers to Beliefs 3, 5 and 6 fall out as the natural residue of having been, for once, actually heard.
